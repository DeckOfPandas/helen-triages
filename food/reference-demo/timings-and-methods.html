Every method in detail

Why some of them refuse

52 of the 66 method rows in the data are a straight formula — minutes per kilo, sometimes plus a flat addition — and multiplying is all they need. The other 14 are five different shapes, and the tempting thing is to coerce them into a number anyway so every row has an answer.

That would be the worst thing this could do. A confident “out at 17:05” derived from a row whose own sources disagree threefold isn’t helpfulness, it’s laundering uncertainty into precision. So the ones that can’t be computed say so, show their real range, and hand you back to the thermometer — pick Duck above and look at the spatchcocked row for the clearest case.

The rows that can be resolved but don’t own their figures — ham’s sugar-rubbed and glazed variants, which the table defines as “same as plain equivalent” — name the row they borrowed from, so the number stays traceable.

See also: the temperature ruler, which is where every “done at” figure here comes from.
