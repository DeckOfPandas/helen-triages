Internal Temperatures

Pull temperatures and endpoints, protein by protein. "Pull at" is the temperature to take something off the heat; the figure after rest is what it actually reaches once carryover cooking finishes the job — the two are not the same number, and going by the resting figure while it's still in the oven will overcook it.

  • Poultry (chicken, turkey, goose, duck)
  • Beef
  • Pork
  • Lamb
  • Ham (fresh and cured)
  • Steak

Poultry

All whole birds go by the thigh, not the breast — the thigh is the last part to come up to temperature.

BirdEndpointCarryoverNotes
Chicken74–75°C in the thigh1–3°CSmaller carryover than a large roast — poultry isn't dense enough to keep climbing much after it's out.
Turkey74–75°C in the thickest part of the thigh, not touching bone1–3°CAlso check the breast reaches at least 70°C.
Goose74–75°C in the thigh1–3°CRest a full 20–30 minutes regardless — goose is fatty enough that this matters more than the temperature reading does.
Duck (whole bird)74–75°C in the thigh1–3°CCooking a whole duck as one piece means accepting the breast will be more well-done than it would be cooked alone — see below.
Duck breast (cooked separately)57–60°C for pink meat, then rested1–3°COnly applies when a breast is cooked on its own, off the bone — not the figure to use for a whole roast bird.

Beef

Tender roasting cuts (rib roast, ribeye roast, round/topside, sirloin roast) — go by doneness temperature, not "until tender."

DonenessPull atAfter ~15–20 min rest
Rare48–50°C~52–54°C
Medium-rare52–54°C~56–58°C
Medium58–60°C~62–64°C
Well done68–70°C~72°C

Carryover on a large roasting joint: 4–6°C — bigger than poultry's, because there's more hot mass still driving heat inward after it leaves the oven.

Tough/working cuts (brisket, chuck, short rib, pot roast) don't work this way at all — they pass through 60–70°C while still tough, and only become tender once collagen breaks down, around 90–96°C. Test with a fork or skewer, not a thermometer alone.

Pork

Roasting cuts (loin, leg/fresh ham, rack/crown roast, tenderloin):

DonenessPull atAfter ~15 min rest
Medium (slightly pink, standard today)60–62°C~63–65°C
Well done68–70°C~71–72°C

Carryover: 3–4°C.

Slow-cooked cuts (shoulder/pork butt, belly) are cooked well past the "done" temperature until fat and collagen render — tender by around 90–95°C. Go by how easily a fork twists in the meat, not a fixed time or a doneness temperature.

Lamb

Roasting cuts (leg, rack, saddle, loin):

DonenessPull atAfter ~15 min rest
Rare50–52°C~54–56°C
Medium-rare54–57°C~58–60°C
Medium60–63°C~63–65°C
Well done68–70°C~71–72°C

Carryover: 3–4°C.

Slow-cooked cuts (shoulder, breast) — same logic as pork's slow-cooked cuts: tender by around 90–95°C, tested by fork, not thermometer alone.

Ham

Fresh (uncured, raw) ham — this is just pork leg, so the same base logic as pork's roasting cuts applies:

Pull at 60–62°C, rest to 63–65°C for a hint of pink, or pull at 68–70°C for well done. Carryover ~3–4°C.

Cured (and/or smoked) ham that still needs cooking — check packaging first: "cook before eating" and "ready to eat" are handled differently, and this figure is for a raw-cured ham that needs cooking:

Target 68–72°C at the thickest point. Carryover ~3–4°C.

Cured ham labelling varies a lot (unsmoked vs smoked, wet vs dry-cured, cook-before-eating vs ready-to-eat) — check the packaging instructions against the thermometer reading rather than relying on a generic figure alone.
