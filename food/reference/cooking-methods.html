Method summary:

  • Pan-sear: hot pan, high smoke-point fat, short cook, rest after
  • Grill/BBQ: direct high heat, similar timing logic to pan-sear
  • Reverse sear: low oven (100–120°C) until close to target temp, then a very hot short sear for the crust — best for thick cuts
  • Pan-sear + oven finish: sear both sides on the stove, then finish in a hot oven (200–220°C)

Doneness pull temps: see internal temperatures.

Cross-cutting methods (apply across several fish/forms above):

  • En papillote: most white fish fillets (cod, haddock, sea bass, sea bream, plaice)
  • Deep-frying (battered): cod, haddock, plaice fillets
  • Ceviche/raw: sea bass, sea bream, tuna

Timing rule of thumb (governed by thickness, not weight): pan-fry/grill ~3–4 min per cm thickness per side; oven-bake at 220–230°C ~4 min per cm thickness total (~10 min per inch). Endpoint is opaque and flaking — see the FOOD SAFETY note below on what temperature that actually corresponds to. Salmon and tuna are commonly pulled well before that for a rare or pink centre; see internal temperatures for both.

Correction, 2026-08-12: the oven-bake rule used to say "~10 min per cm" — this looks like the well-known Canadian "10-minute rule" (10 min per inch of thickness at 450°F/232°C) with inches silently read as centimetres, which makes the old figure roughly 2.5× too long. A 3 cm fillet needs ~12 min by the correct rule, not ~30.

FOOD SAFETY: the "opaque and flaking" endpoint most home cooks actually use is lower than the official benchmark — USDA gives 145°F/62.8°C, and UK FSA guidance points to the same ~63°C. Salmon pulled at 52–55°C for a moist centre (a common chef preference — see internal temperatures) is below that mark; it's a texture choice, not the safety figure, and worth knowing the difference between the two rather than treating "looks done" as equivalent to "meets the guideline."

Sources: pan-fry/grill rate broadly confirmed against UK fishmonger guidance (~2–3.3 min/cm/side for 2–3 cm portions — our figure sits at the high end but same order of magnitude). Oven-bake correction from the Canadian Dept. of Fisheries "10-Minute Rule," the widely-cited basis for most timing guidance of this kind. Safety endpoint from USDA FSIS and UK FSA. Species/method pairings (salt-baked bream, trout amandine, etc.) were all independently confirmed, no changes there.
