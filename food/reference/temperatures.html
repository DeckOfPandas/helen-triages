FSA wants pork held at 60°C for 45 min, 65°C for 10 min, 70°C for 2 min, or 75°C for 30 sec — held, not passed through as it cools. Only the well-done figure clears that.

Venison: tender roasting cutscooking methods →

loin and haunch share their out-at figures — what differs is how far they drift after

{% include food/temp_key.html rested=true carry=true %} {% comment %} TWO CHARTS, ONE SECTION, and the second one is the argument for drawing this at all. UK game sources publish core temperatures for venison rather than per cut, so both charts start from the same out-at ranges; the haunch's dotted stretches are visibly longer because a 1.8 kg joint carries 4–6°C out of the oven where a 600 g loin carries 2–3. Same numbers going in, different meat coming out. {% endcomment %}

{% comment %} NO SHADED ZONE ON EITHER CHART, and that is a decision rather than an omission -- FSA treats a whole cut of venison as it treats beef and lamb, so there is no line to draw. The note below is the mince exception, which is a different question and not one a thermometer answers. {% endcomment %}

Pink venison is fine for the same reason pink beef is: the bacteria are on the surface and the sear kills them. Venison mince is the exception — mincing moves the surface into the middle, and FSA's advice for a burger made from anything other than beef is to cook it through.
