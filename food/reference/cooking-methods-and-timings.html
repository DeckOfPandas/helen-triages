By species, not by weight

Fish and shellfish are not in the calculator above and will not be: fish is governed by its thickness and shellfish by the moment the shell opens, so there is no weight to schedule from. What they do have is options, which is what these two tables are for.

Cross-cutting methods (apply across several fish/forms above):

  • En papillote: most white fish fillets (cod, haddock, sea bass, sea bream, plaice)
  • Deep-frying (battered): cod, haddock, plaice fillets
  • Ceviche/raw: sea bass, sea bream, tuna

Timing rule of thumb (governed by thickness, not weight): pan-fry/grill ~3–4 min per cm thickness per side; oven-bake at 220–230°C ~4 min per cm thickness total (~10 min per inch). Endpoint is opaque and flaking — see the FOOD SAFETY note below on what temperature that actually corresponds to. Salmon and tuna are commonly taken out well before that for a rare or pink centre; see internal temperatures for both.

Correction, 2026-08-12: the oven-bake rule used to say "~10 min per cm" — this looks like the well-known Canadian "10-minute rule" (10 min per inch of thickness at 450°F/232°C) with inches silently read as centimetres, which makes the old figure roughly 2.5× too long. A 3 cm fillet needs ~12 min by the correct rule, not ~30.

FOOD SAFETY: the "opaque and flaking" endpoint most home cooks actually use is lower than the official benchmark — USDA gives 145°F/62.8°C, and UK FSA guidance points to the same ~63°C. Salmon taken out at 52–55°C for a moist centre (a common chef preference — see internal temperatures) is below that mark; it's a texture choice, not the safety figure, and worth knowing the difference between the two rather than treating "looks done" as equivalent to "meets the guideline."

Sources: pan-fry/grill rate broadly confirmed against UK fishmonger guidance (~2–3.3 min/cm/side for 2–3 cm portions — our figure sits at the high end but same order of magnitude). Oven-bake correction from the Canadian Dept. of Fisheries "10-Minute Rule," the widely-cited basis for most timing guidance of this kind. Safety endpoint from USDA FSIS and UK FSA. Species/method pairings (salt-baked bream, trout amandine, etc.) were all independently confirmed, no changes there. Checked once, 2026-08-12 — tidy/remove these notes once you've confirmed a row yourself.
