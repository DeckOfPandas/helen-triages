Issue #469. ROUND 3, 2026-09-01. Helen, having settled the lettering in round 1 and looked at the card in round 2: "Ahhhhh you know what we need to do....? We need to go black background. And pick up all the pieces we break on the way!" She named the shortlist herself (2a, 2c, 3a, 3c), the two title treatments a black panel would want, and the alternative worth taking seriously -- white cards on a black page, with the filter section finding a way to be white on black. She also asked that 2d, the incremental evolution, be considered properly rather than swept along. SO THE PAGE IS SIX STAGES, and each is a whole page ground rather than a card variant. A stage paints its ground past its own box with a box-shadow spread and a clip-path, so a black stage and a light one can sit on one page without a full-bleed rule that could force horizontal scroll. A light ground, 2d cards -- the natural evolution, with filters B BLACK ground, WHITE cards, black tape title -- with filters C black ground, ink cards, NO TAPE, white punched letters D black ground, ink cards, WHITE TAPE, black punched letters E black ground, ink cards, the current black tape -- what "invisible tape" actually looks like, which is the thing worth checking rather than assuming F black ground, ink cards, solid absinthe panel -- the loudest Filters appear in A and B only. The filter treatment is a property of the GROUND, not of the card, so demonstrating it once on light and once on black answers it for every stage below. THE ONE STRUCTURAL FACT, CHECKED RATHER THAN FEARED. A black cocktails does NOT fork the shared chrome. `test_the_header_and_footer_are_identical_on_every_page` compares rendered HTML and colour is CSS; `test_every_chrome_class_has_a_rule_in_every_site_stylesheet` checks DIVERGENCE -- a chrome class styled on one site must be styled on the other -- and never compares values. The chrome already differs in colour between the sites through `$color-accent`, which is exactly what §2.3's palette contract exists to allow. So this is a palette change, not an architecture one. AND `.on-dark` IS ON EVERY BLACK STAGE, which is #469 answered by the largest possible route. Those six custom-property values were tuned in August against a swatch that has since been deleted and have never been rendered on real content; every punched heading inside a dark stage here is running them. But the finding underneath is that a dark SITE may not want the class at all: the class exists for a dark SECTION on a light site, whereas inverting the palette makes every shared partial follow for free -- including `shared/_base.scss`'s hardcoded `$color-text` stroke on plain h1/h2/h3, which is #469's "known gap" and simply stops being one if `$color-text` is itself near-white. WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY BREAK, and none of it is on this page yet: - every `-deep` accent is solved to 4.75:1 against PAPER. On black they are too dark to read; the BARE hues are the text colours on a dark ground. - every `-wash` is a 10% tint of paper, so on black each becomes a near-white block. All five want re-deriving downward. - `_sass/cocktails/_print.scss` must keep printing on white, and `test_print_neutralises_the_screen_page_background` is the guard that already exists for exactly this. - the tape SVGs are drawn black. Stage D inverts them in CSS with `filter: invert(1)`, which is honest enough to look at and wrong in detail: it flips the bevel's light source too. A real white tape is a `scripts/generate_tape.py` run with its colours parameterised. THE PINK IS FOOD'S HOT MAGENTA, #FF00C8, per Helen's round-2 ask. On black it is a different argument than on paper -- it needs no darkening at all, where on paper it needed #C4009A to clear AA. That is one of the pieces going black picks up rather than breaks.
