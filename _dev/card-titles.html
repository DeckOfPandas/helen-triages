    Three things I could not settle by looking

    • The tilt. The wordmark rotates its tape −1.75°. A card is overflow: hidden, so a tilted tape is cut off square by the card edge instead of hanging past it. That is a fact about the card rather than about the idea, and it needs a real decision (drop the tilt, or open the card's overflow and accept whatever else escapes) rather than a number. Not built above, because it changes the card and the title at once.
    • The ellipsis. .drink-card-name ellipsises with text-overflow, which only works on the text directly inside the overflowing box. A tape wrapping the words breaks that, so the Bellini's title is clipped in every row above rather than getting its "…". The SPAN variant is the shape that fixes it, and it is not built either, for the reason below.
    • Hug versus span. A tape hugging the word gives four different tape widths on four cards; a tape spanning the text column gives one edge and stops looking like a torn label. I have built hug only, because if the whole idea is wrong the second axis never matters — and if it is right, that is one more question to put in front of you rather than guess at.
    • The tape is taller than the words it replaces, so the ingredient line sits lower on every variant row than on the control. The card's height is fixed and its foot is pinned, so that space comes out of the ingredients. Left visible rather than compensated for: if the idea survives, how much room the tape is allowed to take is the next question, and hiding the cost now would only move it.
    • The tape SVG is drawn at 1400×170 and stretches with preserveAspectRatio="none", as it does in the header. The header renders it 70px tall; a card title is roughly half that, so the machine marks and the edge bevel are being asked to work at a size they were never chosen at. If the shape reads muddy rather than the idea reading wrong, that is a scripts/generate_tape.py question — a card-sized tape set — not a reason to abandon it.
