Issue #469. ROUND 5, 2026-09-01. Helen: "One thing for sure: we're going black on black. I wear black on black animal print whenever possible. I have some black on black bed linen. This is what we're doing." SO THE LOSERS ARE GONE, per the comparison-switch convention rather than left compiling: the light ground, the white cards, the solid absinthe panel, the no-tape lettering, the white tape and both of its black-letter treatments, the glass-hero and ruled cards, and the sunken E3. Recover any of them from git; they are in rounds 2, 3 and 4 with the reasoning that produced them. WHAT SURVIVED AND WHY: E2 the tape LIFTED off a darker card. Helen: "love it. Black label tape doesn't store well (from my memories of the 80s and 90s...), and does lighten, especially if the roll is manhandled." E4 the tape's rim LIT, both grounds untouched. "Good. Maybe the top edge is a little too heavy -- it's trying to get involved with the text." E3 cut. "E3 blends everything in a way, and just doesn't excite me." ROUND 5 DOES FOUR THINGS. 1. THE TITLE NOW WEARS THE HEADING'S OWN STROKE. Helen: "The YOLO/MOOD etc you've done has outlines over the top edge of the letter. I think this helps both legibility and the raised effect. Please treat the title of cocktails the same way." That outline is `-webkit-text-stroke` in `--emboss-stroke-c`, which `.on-dark` re-points to a mid grey -- darker than a near-white letter, so it cuts an edge into it. The tape lettering had the two-copy shadow and no stroke at all, which is 13.4.1's split with one half missing: STROKE SETS THE EDGE'S WEIGHT, SHADOW SETS ITS DIRECTION. It now reads the same two custom properties the headings do, so it is literally the same treatment rather than a copy of it. Stage `S` is a weight ladder across its four cards -- none, the heading's own 0.010em, 0.016em, 0.024em -- because "the same" and "the same at this size" are different claims and only looking settles which she wants. 2. THE GROUND LIFTS, three ways. "The background a little lighter even, to give the cards more drama, but not so light as to feel connected to the tape." Three tiers have to stay separated: the CARD is the darkest thing, the PAGE sits above it, and the TAPE stays above both. So as the page lifts, the tape's brightness lifts with it -- otherwise the gap she is protecting closes from the other side. 3. THE TAPE WEARS. Her riff, taken literally: a manhandled roll does not lighten uniformly, so stage E2c varies each card's tape by position and adds a little warmth and a little less contrast -- worn black tape goes grey-brown, not grey. Deterministic by card position, so it is a texture rather than a lottery. 4. E4'S TOP EDGE BACKS OFF, two ways -- a lighter highlight, and the same highlight with more room between the tape's rim and the cap line, since "trying to get involved with the text" is as much a distance problem as a weight one. AND `X` IS THE ONE SHE HAS NOT SEEN: E2 and E4 together. A lifted tape with a lit rim on the lifted ground, which is the obvious combination of the two things she liked and was in neither round. PROTANOPIA IS PARKED, at her call, and raised as its own issue rather than left in a commit message -- she named the right shape for it herself ("we could have a protanopia mode. We don't need to limit ourselves to pink for that"), and a mode is a better answer than a compromised hue. The measurement that prompted it is in round 4's commit and in tmp/a11y.py. Four real drinks, 8 / 19 / 24 / 48 characters. Tape shapes deterministic by card position.
