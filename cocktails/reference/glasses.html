A SPEC, NOT AN INVENTORY. #295 asked for "a dictionary of glasses I own with their volumes"; Helen, 2026-09-28: "I don't want inventory, just spec. I might buy new glasses (okay I certainly will). So this is what each glass type holds ON THE MARKET, and nothing about the cupboard. HER BRIEF, 2026-09-29, AND IT IS THE WHOLE PAGE: "show all the glasses together, at relative scale, giving the range and mean ml for each (given the medians are similar we don't need to give those). Don't show the heights. Don't show anything else on the page body. Style GLASSES like a heading. And let's try the glasses in green." So: no intro, no notes, no sources line, no links to drinks. The sources live in `_data/cocktails/glass_survey.yml`, one per product, for anyone who asks. EVERY NUMBER IS A LOOKUP. The drawing is `_includes/icons/glasses/`, its height `heights_mm` (the surveyed median, or Helen's override), the ml `typical_ml` -- all generated from the survey by scripts/glass_survey_stats.py. Nothing here is typed. RELATIVE SCALE IS `_dev/glasses.html` SECTION 2's, the view Helen asked for by name ("I love the side by side view on the ref page, the version based on real glasses"): each glass's real height over the tallest real glass, every glass standing on one baseline per row. ORDERED BY WHAT IT HOLDS, smallest first, so the page reads as a scale from the shot to the punch bowl. Liquid cannot sort by a computed number, so each row is a zero-padded key ("000225|coupe|coupe") sorted as a string. THE MULE MUG IS HERE AS ITS OWN GLASS, drawn as the mug it shares a drawing with, because it is its own size: `survey_only_types` in glasses.yml. THE NAME is the spelling a drink writes -- the first `icons:` key for that drawing that `canonical_glasses` does not correct -- so the page says "double old fashioned" and "brandy glass", exactly the words on a drink page.
