My Philosophy of Rum

{% comment %} Helen's words, not to be polished. `ref-prose` caps the measure; em dashes, not "--", because `test_prose_page_typography` requires them. {% endcomment %}

I really really like rum.

{% comment %} The about page's numbered-list styling, at Helen's request. Her words are unchanged, including item one ending "which informs" and item two starting lowercase: it is one sentence down the column. {% endcomment %}

My rum philosophy cares about only two things:

  1. How a rum tastes, which informs
  2. how to use it in a cocktail.

What colour a rum is tells me very little about how it tastes. How a rum was made tells me a little, but not much, about how it tastes — "pot still" is like saying you like cheese, which can be true, but then you're surprised when someone hands you gorgonzola instead of cheddar. Other boring trainspotting facts like "made in a distillery owned by this particular old white guy" don't interest me at all, and, again, tell me very little about how a rum tastes. The history of Caribbean colonialism is of course in every bottle on my rum shelf, but still tells me — wait for it — very little about how a rum tastes.

{% comment %} Helen's paragraph, verbatim. The list stays whole, and the last sentence ends on "taste" so the next paragraph hangs off it. {% endcomment %}

Rum tastes like sunshine. Human ingenuity has applied itself with zeal to making alcohol from every part of sugarcane: the juice, the syrup, the molasses, even the skimmings. And, like wine, rum tastes like the ground it grows in and what grows around it. The result is endless: sweet, fruity, warm, deep, rich, buttery, toasted, spicy, floral, dry, earthy, grassy, salty, woody, funky, bouncy, sticky, banana, orange, coconut, pineapple, mango, cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, cloves, fire, petrol, coffee, toffee, caramel, vanilla, honey, brown sugar, white sugar, sugar sugar. How can you squash all of that down to "gold coloured" like it means anything about taste?

And if you don't care how a drink tastes, why on earth are you going to all the sticky, impractical effort of making mixed drinks?
