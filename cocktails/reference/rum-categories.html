My rum philosophy cares about only two things:

  1. How a rum tastes, which informs
  2. how to use it in a cocktail.

What colour a rum is tells me very little about how a rum tastes. Boring trainspotting facts like "pot still" or "made in a distillery owned by this particular old white guy" don't interest me at all, and, again, tell me very little about how a rum tastes. The history of Caribbean colonialism also tells me — wait for it — very little about how a rum tastes.

Rum tastes like sunshine. Human ingenuity has applied itself with zeal to making alcohol from sugar cane. Like wine, rum tastes like the ground it grows in and what grows around it. Sweet, fruity, warm, deep, rich, buttery, toasted, spicy, floral, dry, earthy, grassy, salty, woody, funky, bouncy, sticky, banana, orange, coconut, pineapple, mango, cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, cloves, fire, petrol, coffee, toffee, caramel, vanilla, honey, brown sugar, white sugar, sugar sugar, it's just endless. How can you squash all of that down to "gold coloured" like it means anything about taste?

And if you don't care how a drink tastes, why on earth are you going to all the sticky, impractical effort of making mixed drinks?
