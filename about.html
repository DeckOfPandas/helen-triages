    I built this site to solve two problems:

    1. I want to cook something that I already know how to make.
    2. I want to browse recipes I haven't tried yet without having to
      • be able to find a magazine/email/screenshot/bookmark or open tab, or
      • scroll through pages of boring nonsense about your favourite sauté pan or how great your children are.

    This site tells me what to cook. It does not tell me much about how to cook, because I can already cook. So if you want instructions on how to thicken a sauce, or how to sear meat properly, then I suggest you look elsewhere.

      Can I see your to-try recipes?

      No. They only build locally and are committed to a private repository. Three reasons. I don't want to break copyright. I don't want to disrespect authors. And, above all else, I don't want to put effort into rewriting a load of recipes I haven't tested because many will turn out to be terrible.

      I have a great idea/recipe/criticism for your site. Will you build/try/hear it?

      No. I welcome pull requests with thanks. But I'm not interested in pleasing you, in the same way you're not interested in this one time I made courgette jam for a picnic, or memories evoked when my house smells of bread.

      The site looks naff on my phone

      I cook from my 13-inch iPad.

      Did you write any of this code yourself directly?

      Absolutely not. Not one single line, for better or worse, mostly better. I certainly wouldn't have >16,000 tests if left to my own devices. Every time I found myself about to read some YAML, I requested new tests instead. Obviously TDD has always been great, but I'm feeling particularly fond of it now I neither have to write the test nor implement the ask.

      Can you share the prompts or scripts or workflows you used to ingest and clean your recipe data?

      No. I didn't log these things or think them through that hard...welcome to the future: it looks Claude-y. I don't have Repeatable Analysis Pipelines, I have Claude Code, a comprehensive herd of tests, and slightly too much time on my hands at the moment (but still not enough to read YAML).

      Can you write a blog post about what you learned about AI-assisted development?

      Yes, I can. I expect that I will not.
